# Landscape Map

The **Learned Social Simulation Engine** ecosystem represents a shift from static, rule-based modelling to dynamic, data-driven simulation of complex human systems. This map outlines the structural landscape of the field, from foundational theories to practical implementation.

## The Modular Ecosystem

Rather than a single monolithic simulator, the landscape is composed of **bounded system slices**. These are modular, composable models representing specific social domains.

- **Labour Markets:** Modelling employment dynamics, skill transitions, and wage evolution.
- **Urban Mobility:** Simulating transport patterns, infrastructure usage, and city-scale movement.
- **Information Diffusion:** Tracking the spread of ideas, beliefs, and misinformation through networks.
- **Institutional Behaviour:** Representing how organisations, regulators, and governments respond to change.

## Conceptual Layers

The landscape is structured across several layers of abstraction:

1.  **Foundations:** Computational Social Science (CSS) and Complex Systems theory provide the mathematical and sociological basis for emergence and feedback.
2.  **Dynamics:** The mechanisms of change, including Agent-Based Modelling (ABM), Microsimulation, and Machine-Learned World Models.
3.  **Intervention:** The layer where policy and scientific proposals are tested via Causal Inference and counterfactual logic.
4.  **Trust:** Uncertainty Quantification (UQ), validation, and ethical risk discovery ensure the simulations are reliable and responsible.

## Integration with Frontier Science

The field aligns with broader trends in **AI for Science**, adopting methods from:
- **Virtual Cells:** Modelling biological complexity through latent dynamics.
- **Differentiable Simulation:** Enabling gradient-based optimisation of simulation parameters.
- **Generative Agents:** Using Large Language Models (LLMs) to represent complex human behaviour and language-mediated interaction.

## How this applies to this repository

This repository serves as a map for navigating this landscape. Each section of our [Resource Map](../README.md#resource-map) corresponds to a layer or method described here. We curate resources that advance the science of **learned simulation**—moving away from the administrative government tools toward institution-led research that pushes the frontier of AI for social science.
