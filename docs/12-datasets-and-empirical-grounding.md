# Phase 12 — Datasets and Empirical Grounding: Research Notes

**Status:** Private — local only, never to be pushed or made public
**Date:** 2026-05-10
**Last revised:** 2026-05-10 (AI-lab-canonical re-curation)

These notes record candidate datasets and editorial decisions that did not survive the cut into the canonical `### Datasets and Empirical Grounding` section of `README.md`. The README is authoritative; this file is supplementary research context for future review passes (Phase 15 cross-cutting gaps and Phase 16 reference-architecture update).

The curation was re-pivoted on 2026-05-10 from a microsimulation-substrate framing (UKHLS, KLIPS, LIS, LSMS-ISA, ESS, ELSA — research-grade household / labour-microdata panels used by IFS / KLI / LSE / World Bank / Continental European microsimulators) toward an **AI-lab-canonical framing** — datasets that DeepMind, Stanford HAI, Anthropic, Meta FAIR, and Microsoft Research actually cite when grounding generative-agent simulators, LLM-society models, learned-policy audits, and recommender-simulation work. The previous curation is preserved in this notes file under *Candidates considered but not curated* with reasoning for re-evaluation at Phase 15.

## Candidates considered but not curated

### Group 1 — Datasets used by classical microsimulation but not by top AI labs

These are research-grade and would survive a microsimulation-substrate cut, but DeepMind, Stanford HAI, Anthropic, Meta FAIR, and Microsoft Research do not cite them when grounding their generative-agent simulators or LLM-society work. They were curated in the initial pass and removed under the AI-lab-canonical re-pivot.

- **UKHLS / Understanding Society — University of Essex ISER.** Strong UK household panel; canonical substrate for IFS / Resolution Foundation / academic-economist microsimulation tax-benefit work. Not curated under the AI-lab-canonical cut because top AI labs do not cite UKHLS for generative-agent or LLM-society simulator grounding; PSID is the comparable Anglosphere entry that AI labs (Stanford, MIT, NBER) do cite. Re-evaluate at Phase 15 if the cut shifts back toward microsimulation-substrate framing.
- **KLIPS — Korea Labour Institute (KLI).** Strong Korean household panel. Not curated under the AI-lab-canonical cut because the canonical East-Asian AI-lab-cited dataset is CHARLS (China, aging-policy), not KLIPS (Korea, labour). KLIPS-housed at KLI Korea was the strict-reading non-Western anchor under the previous curation; its removal is the principal reason the strict-reading non-Western floor reduces from 2 to 1.
- **LIS (Luxembourg Income Study) Database — LIS Cross-National Data Center (Luxembourg).** Canonical cross-country harmonised income microdata; substrate for Phase 11 fairness-audit work. Not curated under the AI-lab-canonical cut because top AI labs cite OpinionQA / PRISM / GSS for distributional / attitudinal-pluralism grounding rather than LIS for cross-country income-distribution grounding. Re-evaluate at Phase 15 if cross-country distributional-analysis substrate becomes the explicit theme.
- **LSMS-ISA — World Bank LSMS research division.** Multi-country sub-Saharan Africa labour / income panels. Not curated under the AI-lab-canonical cut because top AI labs cite IPUMS-International for non-Western synthetic-population grounding and WVS / PRISM for non-Western attitudinal grounding; LSMS-ISA's sub-Saharan-Africa specialisation is closer to development-economics microsimulation than to AI-lab-canonical simulator substrate. Re-evaluate at Phase 15 with priority on sub-Saharan-Africa AI-native simulator coverage.
- **ESS (European Social Survey) — ESS ERIC at City, University of London.** Canonical European cross-country attitudinal substrate. Not curated under the AI-lab-canonical cut because top AI labs cite WVS (broader cross-cultural coverage) and PRISM (participatory multi-stakeholder) for attitudinal grounding; ESS's European specialisation is incremental on top of WVS for the AI-native simulation use case. Re-evaluate at Phase 15 if European-specific attitudinal grounding becomes the explicit theme.
- **ELSA (English Longitudinal Study of Ageing) — UCL.** Strong UK aging panel; HRS sister study. Not curated under the AI-lab-canonical cut because top AI labs cite HRS (US anchor) and CHARLS (China arm) for aging-policy simulators; ELSA's UK arm is incremental on the HRS-international triad for the AI-native simulation use case. Re-evaluate at Phase 15.

## Editorial decisions taken during curation

- **AI-lab-canonical pivot.** The curation pivoted on 2026-05-10 from microsimulation-substrate framing to AI-lab-canonical framing. The test for an entry is now: *do DeepMind, Stanford HAI, Anthropic, Meta FAIR, or Microsoft Research cite this dataset when grounding their generative-agent simulators, LLM-society models, learned-policy audits, or recommender-simulation work?* Each curated entry's one-liner explicitly names which top-AI-lab paper or simulator cites it. Microsimulation-substrate datasets that fail this test (UKHLS, KLIPS, LIS, LSMS-ISA, ESS, ELSA) are deferred to *Candidates considered but not curated* with reasoning for re-evaluation at Phase 15.

- **Strict non-Western floor relaxation acknowledged.** The previous curation met the ≥2 strict-reading non-Western floor with KLIPS (Korea Labour Institute) + CHARLS (Peking University). The AI-lab-canonical pivot drops KLIPS, leaving only CHARLS as a strict-reading non-Western entry. The trade-off is acknowledged: top AI labs are concentrated at US / UK / Continental European institutions and cite primarily US-housed substrates (PSID, GSS, ANES, MovieLens, OpinionQA) plus US-housed cross-country layers (IPUMS-International, PRISM with multi-lab co-authorship). Non-Western *coverage* is preserved through three multi-country resources (WVS, IPUMS-International, PRISM) and one strict non-Western lab housing (CHARLS). If the strict-reading floor is treated as a hard constraint, CFPS (Peking University ISSS) is the strongest deferred candidate to restore it; the user's editorial choice was to stay at 12 entries with the AI-lab-canonical pivot rather than expand.

- **AI-native usage flagged in every one-liner with explicit top-lab citation where canonical.** Each one-liner names which AI-native simulation use case the dataset supports AND which top-AI-lab paper / simulator cites it (Park et al. 2024 cites GSS; Argyle et al. 2023 cites ANES; Santurkar et al. 2023 built OpinionQA; Kirk et al. 2024 built PRISM; Anthropic GlobalOpinionQA cites WVS; Microsoft / Meta / Google recommender simulators cite MovieLens; Stanford / MIT / NBER computational-economics work cites PSID; Harvard / MIT / Stanford computational-social-science network simulators cite Add Health; NBER / Stanford SIEPR aging-policy ML cites HRS; cross-cultural aging-policy ML cites CHARLS; cross-cultural population-engine work cites IPUMS-International; the Lovelace 2024 synthetic population already curated in `### Microsimulation` is built on IPUMS-USA).

- **Source-quality boundary held with one explicit borderline-call acknowledgement.** PRISM Alignment Project sits on the boundary between research-grade dataset and AI-industry-collaboration dataset (Cohere, MetaAI, Hugging Face are AI labs / industry, not academic research institutions). It is curated under the explicit reasoning that (a) Oxford Internet Institute is the academic lead, (b) the NeurIPS D&B 2024 peer-review pass holds the research-grade bar, and (c) the multi-stakeholder participatory-alignment methodology is exactly the AI-native simulation grounding the *Decisions* block in `requirements.md` named as in scope. If the source-quality bar is tightened in a future review, PRISM should be the first entry re-examined; the alternative is to swap PRISM for an academic-only equivalent (none currently surfaced at the same NeurIPS D&B 2024 quality bar).

- **Sub-area floors held via dual-coverage interpretation.** Sub-area (c) labour and income microdata is satisfied by PSID (which has detailed income, wealth, intergenerational-transfer, and employment modules — the data the LIS Database harmonises across countries originates in panels like PSID). The *search-category* framing in `requirements.md` explicitly allows entries to satisfy multiple sub-areas. Sub-area (d) behavioural / attitudinal is overweighted (5 attitudinal + 1 behavioural-trace) because that is where top AI labs concentrate their simulation work — generative-agent fidelity, LLM-opinion measurement, value-pluralism alignment.

- **`dataset` type label retained.** Single-word, lowercase, matches existing label conventions. Unambiguous about what the artefact is.

- **No duplication of cross-section synthetic-population *artefacts*.** Lovelace 2024 (`### Microsimulation`), CitySEIRCast (`### Synthetic Populations`), Park 2024 *Generative Agents of 1,000 People* (`### Synthetic Populations`), Twin-2K-500 (`### Synthetic Populations`), and Tencent 1B Personas (`### Synthetic Populations`) are *built artefacts* on top of empirical data, not data sources. The IPUMS-USA entry curated here is the *data layer* underneath the Lovelace 2024 synthetic population — the relationship is named explicitly in the IPUMS-USA one-liner. The GSS entry is the *validation substrate* the Park 2024 generative-agents work cites — the relationship is named explicitly in the GSS one-liner.

- **No overlap creep into Phases 13–14.** LLM-training / preference datasets (HH-RLHF, OpenAssistant, LMSYS Arena), data-loading / harmonisation tools (pyreadstat, IPUMS API client, Stata import), and held-out evaluation environments (MELTING POT) are deferred to Phase 13 / 14. The cut here is *empirical-data infrastructure for grounding the simulated society*, not LLM training data, simulator tooling, or evaluation environments.

- **Flat list, no sub-headings.** All 12 entries sit flat under `### Datasets and Empirical Grounding`, ordered by sub-area (longitudinal panels → census microdata → behavioural / attitudinal → behavioural-trace), with non-Western and cross-country entries (CHARLS, IPUMS-International, PRISM, WVS) interleaved in their natural sub-area home rather than segregated.

- **No cross-reference block at section foot.** Mirrors Phase 10 / 11 per the *Decisions* block in `requirements.md`. The section index at the top of `README.md` is sufficient.

## Open gaps to revisit in Phase 15 (cross-cutting gaps)

- **Strict non-Western lab housing back to ≥2.** The AI-lab-canonical pivot leaves CHARLS as the only strict-reading non-Western entry. The strongest deferred candidates to restore the strict floor: CFPS (Peking University ISSS, Chinese household panel), KLIPS (KLI Korea, labour panel — if microsimulation-substrate framing returns), AI4Bharat datasets (IIT Madras / Mumbai, Indian-language LLM substrate), Masakhane African NLP collective. Phase 15 should treat this as a priority gap.

- **Continental European microsimulation substrate.** UKHLS, ESS, ELSA, SOEP (DIW Berlin), HILDA (Melbourne Institute) are the strongest deferred candidates if the cut shifts back toward microsimulation-substrate framing for any future section.

- **Cross-country labour / income microdata for distributional simulators.** LIS (Luxembourg Income Study) and LSMS-ISA (World Bank LSMS sub-Saharan Africa) are the strongest deferred candidates if cross-country distributional-analysis grounding becomes the explicit theme.

- **Anthropic-specific cross-cultural benchmarks.** GlobalOpinionQA (Anthropic 2023) is named inline in the WVS one-liner but not curated as a standalone entry; revisit if Anthropic-house benchmarks proliferate.

- **Multilingual instruction / alignment datasets.** Aya / Aya-23 (Cohere for AI), Open Assistant Conversations, OASST2, multilingual RLHF datasets remain under-curated as a Phase 12 *empirical grounding* layer. The boundary with Phase 13 (LLM training tooling) is the relevant cut.

- **Behavioural-trace ethics gap.** MovieLens is curated as the canonical recommender-simulation substrate, but the broader digital-trace landscape (Reddit research releases, lab-released social-network behavioural corpora, MIT Reality Mining-style sensor data) remains under-curated due to fragmentation and ethics-distribution constraints. Phase 15 priority.

- **Institutional / organisational data.** No curated entry covers institutional-level data (firms, NGOs, government agencies as units) that an AI-native simulator would need to model institutional dynamics. Named gap in the Phase 12 brief; deferred to Phase 15.

- **Long-run demographic registers.** Decade-and-longer demographic registers (Nordic-style population registers, historical demographic projects) remain out of scope due to access restrictions; the methodological gap is real for multi-decade calibration beyond PSID's 1968+ window. Flagged for Phase 15.

- **Game / cooperative-AI behavioural data.** Diplomacy / WebDiplomacy game logs (CICERO substrate), Welfare Diplomacy (Mukobi et al. NeurIPS 2024), public-goods / trust-game experimental archives — strong AI-native cooperative-AI substrates. Most belong in Phase 14 (Benchmarks and Testbeds) where the evaluation-environment framing is the right home; revisit the boundary if any surface as canonical *data sources* rather than benchmarks.

- **Phase 13 tooling.** Data-loading / harmonisation tools (pyreadstat for Stata / SPSS / SAS imports, IPUMS API client + extract-builder Python wrappers, NORC GSS Data Explorer client, Hugging Face datasets loader patterns for OpinionQA / PRISM, GroupLens MovieLens loaders) are deferred to Phase 13.

- **Phase 14 benchmarks.** Held-out evaluation environments built on top of Phase 12 datasets (held-out PSID wave for multi-decade microsimulation validation; held-out GSS round for generative-agent validation in the Park 2024 style; held-out PRISM conversations for cross-cultural alignment evaluation; MELTING POT and similar multi-agent benchmarks) are deferred to Phase 14.

- **Re-evaluation if curation framing shifts back.** If the cut shifts back toward microsimulation-substrate framing for any future section (e.g., a future *Microsimulation Datasets* sub-section), the deferred entries (UKHLS, KLIPS, LIS, LSMS-ISA, ESS, ELSA) are the canonical candidates to surface first.
